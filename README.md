# webfx-demo-emptiness

A WebGL demo coded with [WebFX](https://github.com/webfx-project/webfx) that illustrates the emptiness of phenomena.

Seen from afar, a sphere looks solid and existing by itself. As the camera approaches, it dissolves and reveals the
smaller spheres it is made of. The camera then dives into one of them, which in turn dissolves into its own parts, and
so on, forever. At no level is there a sphere that exists from its own side, independently of its parts.

## How it works

- **Composition**: a sphere is made of `N` smaller spheres (the *Parts per sphere* setting), packed inside it so they
  fill about 27% of its volume. From far they merge into the appearance of one sphere; from close the space between
  them shows. Each part has its own rotation, so the parts of a part are not just a scaled copy of the same arrangement.
- **Reveal**: each sphere is drawn according to `q = camera distance / sphere radius`. Far away (`q` above the *Reveal
  distance* × 1.35), it is a simple solid sphere and its parts are not even computed. Close (`q` below the reveal
  distance / 1.35), only its parts are drawn. In between, its parts are drawn inside it and the sphere becomes
  transparent (its center first, then its rim), so the parts appear without any visible switch.
- **Infinite zoom**: the camera follows a logarithmic spiral from the sphere to the chosen part, ending when that part
  fills the screen exactly as the sphere did at the start. The coordinates are then rebased (the part becomes the new
  sphere), so the zoom never runs out of floating point precision. The part to dive into is a front part, which the
  camera sees unobstructed during the whole dive, so the camera only flies through empty space (never close to another
  part or stem). Consecutive spirals are blended to avoid a jolt when the target changes.
- **Surroundings**: a few ancestors are kept to draw what surrounds the camera; spheres fade out as they become much
  bigger than the current scale, so the oldest ancestor can be dropped without anything visibly disappearing.

The sphere positions are computed relative to the camera in double precision (Java) and sent to a small WebGL 1 shader
as a center and a radius, so no 3D library is needed.

## Running the demo on your local machine

The webapp is generated by running the following Maven command:

`mvn package -P gwt-compile`

The generated `index.html` file is located at:

`webfx-demo-emptiness-application-gwt/target/webfx-demo-emptiness-application-gwt-0.1.0-SNAPSHOT/webfx_demo_emptiness_application_gwt/index.html`

Open it from your IDE or any web server.
